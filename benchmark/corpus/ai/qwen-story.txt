The wind howled across the jagged cliffs of Blackwood Bay, rattling the lantern room shutters of Keeper Elias Thorne's solitary post. For forty years, he had guarded this beacon, speaking to ghosts rather than people. The ocean was his only visitor, a restless beast that claimed what it could and left the rest to drift.

It was during a storm that felt particularly violent that a bottle tumbled from the waves onto the rotting wooden dock. It was heavy, made of thick, salt-stained glass, and sealed with a waxed cork that had clearly been soaked for decades. Elias picked it up, his boots slipping on wet sludge. As he broke the wax seal, a faint, acidic smell rose to greet him, followed by a crumpled paper note.

He unfolded the message under the flickering gaslight of his office. The handwriting was shaky, hurried, written in ink that looked like spilled rainwater. *"To whoever finds this: I am not lost at sea; I am lost at home. My wife left me three days ago when the tide went out. If you read this, tell her I tried to hold on. Tell her the lighthouse light is still burning."*

Elias felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach, instantly replaced by a strange warmth spreading through his chest. He had seen the faces of men and women wash up on the rocks, but he had never considered that someone might be out here, stranded not by the sea, but by circumstances far more crushing than drowning.

He rolled up the note and placed it carefully inside his leather satchel, next to a small jar of preserved ship's biscuits. The storm outside battered the cliffs with renewed fury, but Elias no longer felt entirely alone. For the first time in forty years, the rhythmic pounding of the waves seemed less like an assault and more like a countdown to something that had been waiting for him all along. He lit his lamp again, knowing that while he could not bring back a wife, perhaps he could guide others who were still at home but felt as if they had been cast adrift.
